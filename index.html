ABOUT THIS EDITION

Principles before labels.

This test explores political commitments in detail, with particular attention to Christian political thought, natural law, the common good, economic institutions, and AI.

For questions about religion, imagine a historically Christian country whose citizens now hold differing beliefs. “The Church” means the ecclesiastical authority recognized by the proposed establishment. Social questions ask about morality unless they expressly concern the law. “Frontier AI” means the most capable general-purpose AI systems.

A working research edition

Progress and submitted result versions are saved in this browser. New or revised questions can be answered as a supplement. Editing and resubmitting recalculates results from your current answers. Download a backup to keep a separate copy; clearing browser data removes local history. The directional bars are provisional answer summaries, not validated scientific measures. Categorical positions remain visible rather than being reduced to an arbitrary midpoint.

Personality, ideology, country, and AI comparisons use documented positions. Expand a match to see its sources, agreements, differences, and limits. Profiles can be eligible in a field while lacking the breadth needed for overall matching. Up to eight overall personalities are shown; missing slots are not filled with unsupported guesses.

Temporary ranking mode

When temporary equalization is enabled, candidates with at least one supported answered position can participate below normal qualification. Every candidate uses the same answered question set; unknown profile evidence contributes a neutral ranking value, without inventing an answer. Explicit evidence moves the rank fully; inferred evidence moves it at 0.65 weight. Overall ranks balance dimensions equally. Religion retains its existing effect. Ranking scores and documented similarity are labelled separately. Disable ENABLED in temporary-ranking.js to restore ordinary ranking.

Normal evidence and scoring

Overall comparisons give each compared primary dimension equal weight. Economics instead averages its compared questions directly, so a cross-field question cannot acquire disproportionate weight just by belonging to another dimension. An explicit underlying position receives weight 1 within its dimension; an interpretive mapping receives weight 0.65. Agreement scales use graded distance, ordered choices use their declared direction, and categorical alternatives use exact agreement. Explicitly supported alternatives use the closest fit. Inferred alternatives use the average fit across the mapped options, with a displayed minimum–maximum interpretation range; this is not a statistical confidence interval. Unknown answers do not count as neutral. Overall people and countries require eight documented dimensions and twelve comparable questions; overall ideologies require six dimensions and twelve questions. All need institutional, social/religious, and economic coverage. Field matches require four comparable questions; AI requires three. The dedicated Economics section separates broader comparisons (at least eight questions across two dimensions) from narrower parallels. All personalities with sufficient field evidence are considered, regardless of their catalog label. Percentages describe similarity only over the evidence compared, so always consider coverage. Differences under three percentage points should be read as near-ties. Historical figures receive no invented AI views. Natural-law reasoning is distinct from judicial discretion; establishment is distinct from direct clerical government.
